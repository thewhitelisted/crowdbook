crowdbook 0.14.0 gives market makers order flow to read, so that a maker can set the price rather
than follow it; fits markets that follow a recorded reference, such as a perpetual future its
index; and stops every market maker meeting its own quotes, which changes how makers behave by
default.

## Install

```bash
pip install crowdbook-engine==0.14.0 crowdbook-analysis==0.14.0
```

From PyPI, as is `crowdbook`, the client for served markets: wheels for Linux (x86-64 and arm64,
manylinux) and macOS (Apple silicon, 14 or later), for Python 3.12 and every later version. Start
with [docs/quickstart.md](https://github.com/thewhitelisted/crowdbook/blob/v0.14.0/docs/quickstart.md)
and [docs/tutorial.md](https://github.com/thewhitelisted/crowdbook/blob/v0.14.0/docs/tutorial.md).

## New

**A market maker that sets the price**
([docs/scenarios.md](https://github.com/thewhitelisted/crowdbook/blob/v0.14.0/docs/scenarios.md),
[docs/design.md](https://github.com/thewhitelisted/crowdbook/blob/v0.14.0/docs/design.md)). Three
`market_maker` settings, each off by default:
- `flow_impact` moves its fair price by each lot of aggressive volume, up for buys and down for
  sells, as in Glosten and Milgrom and in Kyle;
- `flow_memory` lets that impact fade, to 1/e over that long; unset, it stays;
- `take_through` trades against someone else's quotes its fair price has moved past by more than
  that many ticks, up to its inventory limit, rather than waiting behind them.

Known answers check them: the units; a maker reading flow loses less to informed takers and gives
up under 5% to uninformed ones; a finite memory makes the mid revert; and twins fitted to markets
made at known settings find them, `flow_impact` to about a tenth and `flow_memory` to about half.
`level_spacing = 0` puts every level of a maker's ladder at one price, its quote split into orders
as a deep touch is a queue of many.

**Fitting markets that follow a reference**
([docs/validation.md](https://github.com/thewhitelisted/crowdbook/blob/v0.14.0/docs/validation.md)):
- `[fundamental] path`: a recorded value in ticks, `[[seconds, ticks], ...]`, in place of the
  random walk, for any market.
- Index twins: a market that is not a contract on an outcome, such as a perpetual future, is
  twinned on its recorded reference. A tape with a value table and no payout fills
  `${value_path}`, `${opening_value}` and `${opening_price}`, and
  `examples/calibration/index_twins.toml` follows it, with makers that can read flow
  (`index_flow_spec.toml`).
- `value_basis`: a fitted offset between a market and its reference, for one that trades apart
  from it.
- A tape's `lot`, for a venue that trades fractions, and `block`, for one that stamps its trades
  with the time of the block they fall in, so that simulated trades are counted as the venue's
  are.
- A fit keeps its best settings so far in `searching.toml`, so that a fit cut short resumes.

**Getting started**:
- `examples/recording`: an hour of five-level quotes and trades as gzipped CSV, to try
  `crowdbook-tape` on.
- `crowdbook <command> --help` shows that command's usage.
- A release goes on to PyPI by trusted publishing as soon as its tag is pushed.

## Changes that may need yours

- **Market makers behave differently by default.** When a maker's new bid would reach one of its
  own asks as they stand, the asks now move first and the bid after. In 0.13 it moved its bids
  first, and the exchange cancelled a bid that met its own ask as a self-trade, so a maker whose
  fair price jumped past its own ask lost that bid until its next requote. A maker quoting a ladder
  of levels now sends one whole side before the other, so that when its price falls, its new asks
  no longer meet its own bids further down. In a market whose price jumps, a maker lost 29 bids
  that way in 300 seconds, and a two-level ladder 21; now each loses none. A one-level maker whose
  price moves less than its spread between requotes runs exactly as before: the golden runs keep
  their hashes, and the tutorial's fit, its no-false-edge check and the reference twins give the
  same figures. `examples/scenarios/game.toml`, whose makers' price jumps with every score, moves
  slightly. Refit a scenario fitted with 0.13 whose prices jump before comparing results across
  the two.
- Depth within levels is measured only in markets whose tape fills those levels, and depth within
  ticks only where its last level reaches that far nine tenths of the time. A tape of the touch
  alone measured its depth as the touch size before, which a fit aimed its makers at.
- `find_package(crowdbook 0.14)`: until 1.0 only the same minor version is compatible.
- Scenario files, session files and the protocol keep their version numbers, and sessions
  recorded by earlier versions still replay.
